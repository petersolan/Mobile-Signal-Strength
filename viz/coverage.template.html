Residents by modelled 4G coverage

Indoor means a modelled signal of at least −95 dBm, enough to reach inside most buildings. Outdoor only is −105 to −95 dBm. These are Ofcom's 4G thresholds.

How good is the model?

Against Ofcom's own prediction

Ofcom's Connected Nations 2025 publishes the operators' own coverage predictions (snapshot 1 July 2025) for Exeter local authority, counted by premises. This model counts residents and uses only open data, so it is the more cautious of the two.

How it works

  • Cells and masts. Each drive-test reading lists every operator's four strongest cells (PCI and EARFCN) with their RSRP. For each cell, a path-loss curve RSRP = A − 10·n·log10(d) is fitted to its readings, binned to 20 m squares, which estimates where its mast is. Positions from road readings are approximate, typically a few hundred metres off.
  • Coverage. On a 25 m grid, each operator's coverage is its strongest modelled cell, shifted by the operator's median difference from the measurements. Near the roads the remaining measured − modelled difference is spread out over about 150 m, so the map matches the drive tests there and follows the model elsewhere.
  • Residents. Census 2021 residents were spread to every address (UPRN) in an earlier project; each address takes the modelled coverage of its 25 m square.
  • Not operator coverage. The model has no terrain, building clutter or antenna patterns, and the drive tests are from 2022–2024. Use it as an estimate from open data, not as an operator's coverage map.
  • Validation. One in five 1 km squares is held out, the whole model is rebuilt without its readings, and those squares are predicted. Errors are in dB against the measured strongest-cell RSRP.
